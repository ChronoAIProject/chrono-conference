## Our Epistemic Discipline: Separating Positions, Data, and Proofs

The deepest embarrassment in engineering is not having unfinished work—every real system has unfinished edges. The embarrassment is painting "done" across a blueprint that has never survived contact with reality. Richard Feynman's first principle was simple: *you must not fool yourself, and you are the easiest person to fool.* Friedrich Nietzsche would add the hammer test: tap every idol lightly, and listen for the hollow ring.

Every book in this conference edition obeys three non-negotiable truth tiers:

1. **Green Lean kernel badges belong strictly to the Lean 4 kernel.** Whenever Chapter 5 or the Coda cites a formal theorem, it resolves against a frozen declaration in `the-omega-institute/trureturing` at pinned commit [{commit}](trureturing:), linking directly to its exact file and line. No green checkmarks are ever hand-typed into prose.
2. **Analogies and strategic stances are labeled as analogies.** "Engine vs. vehicle," "tech tree vs. gold rush," "the tamed Red Queen," and "boundary and bulk" are sharp lenses for reasoning—positions and heuristics, **never** theorems.
3. **Red `OPEN` is reserved strictly for unsolved open problems, while engineering limits are disclosed candidly in plain sight.** Red `OPEN` blocks mark genuine unproved mathematical and scientific frontiers (such as the Riemann Hypothesis itself); in parallel, every unfinished engineering seam—`heca`'s pending public source release, `CMA`'s single-runtime status today, `chrono-harness`'s explicit `completeness_proven: false` specification flag, and `NyxBot` Finance operating on a demo ledger with zero external paying merchants so far—is stated with its exact scope, date, and limit in our prose and appendices.
